I do not see artificial intelligence as something that arrived from beyond. It is, like every technology before it, a construction born from human choice. We built it because we chose to solve problems at a scale our bare hands could not reach.

In my view, the conversation around AI has become too mystical. We speak of it as if it were an entity with its own will, when in reality it is a tool — the highest layer of abstraction we have yet created. It extends our capacity to think, to build, and to fail faster. But it does not replace the need for human judgment.

When I write code with AI, I measure the output against the same criteria I would use for code written alone: Does it behave correctly? Is it secure? Can it scale? Is it maintainable? The tool changes, but the standards do not. People do not care whether you used a language model or a typewriter. They care whether the software works.

The danger is not that AI will replace us. The danger is that we will outsource our responsibility to it. We will stop asking why we build what we build, and simply generate. But purpose cannot be automated. Purpose is what guides the tool, not the other way around.

Technology shapes the way we live. AI is shaping it now. The question is not whether we should use it, but whether we will use it with intention. I choose to use it as a lever — to solve more problems, to learn through failure more quickly, and to retain long value in what I create. Because at the end of it all, the world remains what we choose to make of it.
