## TL;DR

Economics is the study of human action, in relation to the scarcity of goods and services. Markets react to the choices that people make, driven by three core elements: Action (what people do), Purpose (the goals they pursue), and Reason (why they choose one option over another).

These three factors significantly influence how markets for products can exist. What draws a person to purchase a good or service, is the value of the good, to the person. Value is not objective, it is subjective (meaning it depends on what matters to a person). Value is influenced by several factors:

- **Time or Context** — An umbrella is more valuable to a person during the rainy season, than during the summer season. And when you're less likely to buy things a teenager is thinking about.
- **Preferences** — Humans are extremely different, in one way or another. Someone may purchase something that makes no sense to you. For instance, men would pay money to watch a football match that they'll eventually forget about in weeks to come, as the feeling vanishes, and women may find this hilarious, similar to how men would wonder why women would do their hair or nails, just to replace it again a few weeks later.
- **Culture** — Culture influences market in a niche way. For instance, African traditional clothes are more likely to sell in Africa because of heritage, and people could buy them at ridiculous prices just for the sake of culture. Another item relevant to this is of course phones: we buy phones today because of how we communicate today. My parents bought phones for my grandparents and they rarely used them and didn't see any use apart from just calling people, and ended up abandoning them. But it's because their times and ours are different, and of course their culture didn't embrace these things. Different to how not having one today could make people wonder about you.

For a long time, I used to confuse money as the market. But money is just a receipt. It allows us to eradicate the ancient problem of double coincidence of wants. The actual economy is goods and services. Before we dive in deeply, let's look at the origin of trade, which obviously led to the current economic structures we have today.

## The Origin of Trade

It all began with barter trade, where people traded what they had for what they needed, but this was an unsustainable system. Because it was hard to find someone who had what you needed in exchange for what you had (lack of double coincidence of wants). Humans then moved to using minerals such as gold and silver, and other valuable materials, as standards of exchange depending on the community. As civilizations grew into more structured political structures, more centralized methods for exchange were introduced such as the use of coins for exchange which were issued by government institutions.

With further progression, we had paper money which was easier to carry than gold or silver, which we came to term or coin as Fiat Money, referring to money controlled by the government in terms of distribution. This system originated in England, where instead of the people holding gold which was scarce, all they had was trade the gold with the government in exchange for money to hold against the value of their gold, and used that legalized money (by the government) to perform trade. To further understand how the Fiat system came into place, and the evolution of money, read: *The Fiat Standard*, by Saifedean Ammous.

So, the evolution of money as a means of performing trade, allowed people to have more freedom in terms of what they could trade and broke the boundary that long existed. This meant people could buy whatever they found of value from the existing markets.

## The Evolution of Markets

Market is a term that can be used to refer to a place or the state of a good or services having supply and demand. Markets have evolved a lot around technology with the internet paving way for online markets we see today, and we can ask for delivery of goods to our doorsteps.

But my main concern on including this, is how did markets for items like smartphones, come to exist? Why do we buy these things in the first place? They were never of necessity in the first place. The only basic needs that people want are: food, shelter, and clothes. And the primary goods that were for trade in the beginning of barter were food products, where they exchanged food products for survival, and came about when their demands became scarce. I think many reasons or conclusions can be drawn from this and there may be no direct answer, but for most of humanity, what has come to be of today is a result of our natural reaction to the unfolding of the various events that have been occurring.

Human beings traded for what was of value to them, and the same is for today. We trade for what is of value to us. The diversity of goods and services we see today, in my opinion, came when man was able to produce in mass quantity, or in other words we say surplus. Not all environments were conducive enough to produce food for entire years and not all land was enriched with all the resources it needed, and thus man was forced to interact with other communities to meet their needs. We read of the voyages of Vasco Da Gama in search for spice and a route to India for trade.

The industrial revolution opened up doors for more of what people could trade for. Upon the discovery of machines that could mass produce, and people could earn money from the surplus harvest and processed foods, people captivated on that. And to mass produce, people needed more land, and more people to work on these machines. So, if a person could operate the machines to help them in production, the owners would hire them to work on their business.

Again, oil was not valuable because no one knew what it could be used for till when people discovered it could be refined and then used as fuel for cars, trains and of course industrial machines. So, it only came to be of value after people used it and saw that it was needed or what it could be used for.

I hinted of those two scenarios to show that markets come from unrealized demands, problems that are yet to have solutions or not known to the public. People never thought they'd need a phone till it was created and people got to see what it could do, and ditched the old phones. At the pivot of all this, lies technology. Technology significantly influences what we can produce and what we cannot. Some of the services such as streaming music or movies would never exist if it were not for computers and the internet. Of course there are other attributes but all of that existed because of technology enabling them.

## Roles in the Market

For markets to be sustainable they need a perfect or almost near good balance between the supply and demand. Meaning producers need to produce enough quantity to meet the demands of the market at a price that the market will be willing to pay for. When a person makes a purchase, they value the product more than holding the value of that good in terms of cash at the moment. Otherwise, they'd keep their money to buy something else that is worth buying.

## Entrepreneurship

So, we have the role of the supplier. The role of the supplier is to produce goods to meet the demand in the market. Note that, the market does not exist until a good or service is offered to customers who, when they are many, create a strong market. So, if a good does not have enough demand to keep it going it eventually falls or withers away. There are instances where the demand may be large but the supply is small (a few people produce the goods or services or there are simply low distributions). This situation is called scarcity. This forces the supplier to hike the price up to their advantage because of the competition for demand. However, when the demand is high and the supply is also high (meaning many people produce that good or service), the suppliers can be incentivized to sell at lower costs to reach market demand.

Looking into the supplier, we can just refer to them as Entrepreneurs, whose function is to organize the other factors of production to produce goods and services for consumers. Their job is to feed the market with supply. The factors of production are: Land, Capital, Labor. Entrepreneurs take the risk of organizing those factors, to produce goods and services for users. They incur the costs; by doing so they forgo the gains they could get from spending the capital they have, to supply for the market. The reward for their risk is of course profit.

## Land

All of the raw materials that humans use for production originate from land. It is the primary source of all goods. Services however rely on the input of a variety of skills and tools. There are two major types of goods: consumer goods and producer goods. Consumer goods are the goods that you and I consume, while producer goods are the goods that are used to produce other goods. So, from land, we extract things like metals and other minerals that under further processing we come to make goods like computers, machinery and many more. From land we can also grow crops and process that to get consumer goods. An entrepreneur could make producer goods for other people to use to make the final goods, or use these producer goods themselves. So the entrepreneur has to utilize the resources they have to produce.

For the case of raw materials such as gold, depending on their geographical location they may need to interact with the government, obtain license and all legalities to harvest these resources. Land can also be used to build infrastructure such as factories, or they can acquire offices where they can offer you their services (a place to produce the goods or serve you the goods).

## Capital

Capital refers to the financial resources and tools used in production. It is what enables the entrepreneur to acquire land, hire labor, and purchase machinery. Without capital, ideas remain theoretical. With it, they become goods and services that enter the market.
